3. Executive Summary

Urban Fix BD is a specialized, production-ready civic technology platform engineered to bridge the operational gap between citizen-reported infrastructure failures and verified municipal execution in Bangladesh. Metropolitan governance across Bangladesh—specifically within Chattogram City Corporation (CCC), Dhaka North City Corporation (DNCC), and Dhaka South City Corporation (DSCC)—suffers from systemic inefficiencies: manual triage bottlenecks, overlapping agency mandates, undocumented resolutions, and widespread citizen distrust.

Urban Fix BD replaces opaque civic complaint portals with an automated, explainable, and verifiable pipeline. When a citizen submits a photograph and location, the platform deploys a multimodal vision pipeline utilizing Google Gemini (gemini-3.5-flash-lite) to classify the civic problem into standardized municipal categories, assign severity tiers, assess compounding safety hazards, and generate an HMAC-SHA256 preview token. A pure deterministic Bangladesh authority routing engine evaluates geographic area keywords, ray-casted boundary polygons, and institutional responsibility rules to assign complaints to city corporations or specialized utilities (such as CWASA, DWASA, BPDB, DESCO, DPDC, LGED, or RHD) while routing ambiguous boundary cases to human officer review.

Core Engineering Achievement
Urban Fix BD eliminates "ghost resolutions" through a strict two-party verification lifecycle. An issue cannot be marked resolved in the database without field-submitted photographic evidence, authority sign-off, and explicit confirmation from the original citizen reporter via a private cryptographic token. Every state change and administrative override is permanently chained into a tamper-evident SHA-256 audit ledger.
Backend Architecture Metrics

Built with Python 3.11 and FastAPI 0.115.6. Fully tested across 120 automated test cases with a 100% pass rate. Supports transactional MongoDB Motor persistence with zero-dependency in-memory dictionary fallback.

Frontend & Cadastre Design

Built with React 19.2.8 and Vite 8.2.2. Features the "GovTech Civic Trust" high-contrast design system, instant English/Bengali localization, interactive before/after image comparison sliders, and public Leaflet geospatial mapping.

4. Introduction & Platform Mission

Civic infrastructure maintenance in high-density developing nations represents one of the most critical determinants of public health, safety, economic productivity, and governance credibility. In Bangladesh, rapid urbanization across metropolitan corridors has exacerbated municipal service delivery challenges. Potholes turn into lethal vehicle traps during monsoon floods, uncollected waste blocks drainage culverts leading to waterlogging, and open electrical utility lines pose severe electrocution risks.

The mission of Urban Fix BD is to establish an unassailable digital chain of civic accountability. By transforming unstructured mobile uploads into structured municipal work orders, validating geographic jurisdictions deterministically, and securing every lifecycle transition with cryptographic audit pointers, the platform provides public servants with actionable triage data while guaranteeing citizens transparent visibility into the lifecycle of their reports.

4.1 Core Design Principles

  • AI as Decision Support, Never Sole Authority: Artificial intelligence provides fast, structured recommendations; deterministic code and human officers retain final authority on operational dispatch and closure.
  • Zero Ghost Resolutions: No municipal repair can be marked completed based solely on verbal or textual claims. High-resolution photographic evidence and reporter sign-off are structurally required by the finite-state machine.
  • Cryptographic Tamper-Evidence: Administrative tampering is prevented by chaining all status updates into an append-only SHA-256 hash log emitting verifiable public accountability receipts.
  • Privacy by Default: Citizen reporters are protected through automatic regex scrubbing of National Identity (NID) numbers, phone numbers, and emails, coupled with spatial coordinate rounding on all public feeds.
  • Volunteer Safety Safeguards: High-risk infrastructure (high-voltage electricity, gas, structural collapse) is strictly barricaded against youth/community micro-repair claims.

5. Background and Problem Analysis

A rigorous audit of municipal complaint management across Bangladeshi metropolitan authorities reveals five fundamental structural breakdowns:

5.1 Unstructured Data & Triage Overload

Citizens currently report problems through social media posts, telephone calls, or ad-hoc municipal grievance boxes. These reports lack standardized taxonomy, severity assessment, or standardized coordinates. Municipal supervisors spend hours reading through ambiguous complaints without knowing whether an issue is an imminent life-safety threat (e.g., an exposed live wire in a flooded street) or routine maintenance (e.g., street sweeping).

5.2 Jurisdictional Fragmentation & Institutional Silos

In major Bangladeshi cities, municipal territory is segmented across overlapping institutions. For example, in Chattogram, surface drains fall under Chattogram City Corporation (CCC), while underground sewerage pipes and water supply belong to Chattogram WASA (CWASA). Arterial port roads overlap between CCC, the Roads and Highways Department (RHD), and the Chattogram Port Authority (CPA). Power distribution is managed by the Bangladesh Power Development Board (BPDB). Existing grievance portals route blindly, causing inter-agency buck-passing where complaints remain unattended for months.

5.3 The "Ghost Resolution" Phenomenon

Traditional municipal software records resolutions based on internal administrative entries. Field contractors or departmental crews routinely close tickets in the system without performing physical repairs, knowing that the citizen has no mechanism to challenge the status update. This creates a severe trust deficit between urban citizens and public institutions.

5.4 Financial and Privacy Exposure

Public exposure of citizen phone numbers and home addresses on social media or unredacted grievance portals leads to harassment and political intimidation. Simultaneously, deploying municipal crews to false alarms or duplicate reports wastes scarce civic budgets.

9. Non-Functional Requirements Specification

9.1 Performance and Latency

  • AI Preview Inference: Gemini multimodal intake analysis responds within ≤ 3.5 seconds under standard network conditions; times out safely at 90 seconds.
  • Deterministic Routing Execution: Local geographic keyword resolution and polygon ray-casting completes in ≤ 5 milliseconds without external API lookups.
  • Public Map Queries: Spatial clustering endpoint (/api/complaints/map/clusters) processes up to 2,000 incident points and returns clustered centroids in ≤ 80 milliseconds.
  • Frontend Asset Bundling: Vite production build generates minified and gzip-optimized assets in ≤ 9.0 seconds.

9.2 Reliability & Fault Tolerance

  • Dual-Mode Persistence Fallback: If MongoDB is temporarily unreachable, the backend automatically transitions to an in-memory dictionary mirror in development without crashing.
  • AI Fallback Heuristics: If Gemini returns rate limits (HTTP 429), server errors (HTTP 5xx), or invalid JSON, the system triggers _fallback_analysis without interrupting user intake.
  • Idempotency Enforcement: Submissions with duplicate Idempotency-Key headers return existing complaint records without re-executing storage or AI workflows.

9.3 Security and Cryptographic Standards

  • Password Security: Passwords hashed with PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 using 310,000 iterations and a cryptographically secure 16-byte salt.
  • Session Handling: 48-byte URL-safe random session tokens stored as SHA-256 hashes in the database; delivered via HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax cookies.
  • CSRF Mitigation: All mutating HTTP requests (POST, PATCH, DELETE) require an X-CSRF-Token header validated against the active session using constant-time comparison.
  • Audit Trail Immutability: Every audit entry includes event_hash = sha256(canonical_json(event)) linked to previous_hash.

13. Database Architecture & Dual-Mode Persistence

To facilitate development and automated testing, the persistence layer implements a seamless Dual-Mode Persistence Architecture managed by CivicRepository in backend/app/db/repository.py.

13.1 Dual-Mode Operation

  • MongoDB Production Mode: Connects to a standard MongoDB replica set (local or MongoDB Atlas). Executes atomic transactions via async with civic_repo.transaction() as session:. Automatically initializes unique and compound indexes on startup.
  • In-Memory Dictionary Mirror: When ALLOW_MEMORY_FALLBACK=true and MongoDB is unreachable, the system automatically runs using an in-memory dictionary mirror covering all 14 collections. The transaction context manager takes deep snapshots of the memory space and rolls back cleanly if an exception occurs, enabling deterministic, zero-dependency testing.

14. AI Integration & Multimodal Processing Pipeline

The AI subsystem in Urban Fix BD acts as an intelligent decision-support triage assistant. It transforms raw mobile uploads into structured municipal data while strictly adhering to the architectural rule: AI classifies the problem; deterministic code and human officers govern assignment, lifecycle, and closure.

Figure 2.0: Multimodal AI Image Processing & Cryptographic Preview Token Pipeline

14.1 Image Sanitization & Resampling (_optimize_image_for_ai)

Before image bytes are dispatched to Google Gemini, they are sanitized by Pillow:

  • Metadata Stripping: Deletes private EXIF tags containing serial numbers, personal device metadata, or raw timestamps.
  • Orientation Transposition: ImageOps.exif_transpose ensures photos taken upside down or in portrait mode are permanently oriented correctly.
  • Dimension Bounding: Maximum dimension is capped at 1600px using Lanczos antialiasing. Smaller images are never upscaled.
  • Format Normalization: Transformed to RGB JPEG quality 85. Original bytes are preserved untouched for Cloudinary evidence storage.

14.2 Prompt Engineering & Municipal Rules

The prompt supplied to Gemini strictly instructs the model to prioritize photographic evidence over user-typed descriptions, enforce the Bangladesh municipal taxonomy, identify public safety hazards, and reject non-civic media (such as domestic interiors, selfies, or personal documents).

15. Complaint Submission & Intake Workflow

The citizen submission lifecycle follows an idempotent, four-step interaction sequence designed to minimize server load and user friction:

15.1 Step-by-Step Intake Lifecycle

  1. Step 1: Evidence Capture & Preview Call (POST /api/complaints/analyze): The citizen uploads a photo and optional category hint. The server optimizes the image, queries Gemini, and returns the classification preview along with an HMAC-SHA256 analysis_token.
  2. Step 2: Location Confirmation & Review: The citizen reviews the AI classification. Permitted GPS suggestions extracted from photo metadata are displayed on the map. The citizen confirms or adjusts the street area name and GPS pin.
  3. Step 3: Complaint Creation (POST /api/complaints/with-image): The client posts the full form containing the image, area, GPS, description, and analysis_token. The server validates the token signature. If valid and matching the payload binding, the server reuses the previewed analysis, saving 100% of duplicate inference fees.
  4. Step 4: Storage, Routing, and Receipt Issuance: The image is safely stored in Cloudinary (or local uploads). The routing engine resolves the authority. The database inserts the complaint and generates:
    • A unique Public Case ID: CP-XXXXXX (e.g., CP-9082).
    • A private citizen verification token: reporter_verification_token (stored as an SHA-256 hash in report_owners with a 30-day expiration).

15.2 Deduplication & Incident Clustering

Incoming reports are compared against active complaints in the same geographic radius. If an existing incident is detected with confidence ≥ 0.82, the report is linked to the existing incident_id cluster rather than discarded. This increments the incident's duplicate count, boosting its priority score while preserving the new citizen's individual evidence and ownership.

16. AI Classification, Severity & Priority Scoring

18. Complaint Tracking & Status Management

Urban Fix BD models complaint progression via a formal Finite-State Lifecycle Machine implemented in backend/app/services/lifecycle.py.

Figure 4.0: Complete Complaint Lifecycle Finite-State Machine with Dispute Gates

18.1 Transition Invariants

  • Direct transitions from Submitted or In Progress to Resolved are strictly rejected (HTTP 409 Conflict).
  • Transitions to Evidence Uploaded, Verification, or Resolved require non-null resolution_evidence in the database record.
  • Reaching final closure (Resolved) requires fully_verified=True, indicating both contractor/officer photographic evidence submission and municipal verification approval.
  • When a citizen disputes a resolution, status shifts to Reopened - Needs Review with review_type="citizen_dispute", preserving prior evidence in previous_resolution_evidence.

19. Evidence and Verification Workflow

The core innovation separating Urban Fix BD from conventional complaint software is its Two-Party Photographic & Cryptographic Verification Workflow.

19.1 Field Resolution Evidence (POST /api/complaints/{id}/evidence/upload)

When municipal field teams or contractors finish physical repairs on-site, they must upload an after-repair photograph with GPS metadata and completion notes. The backend executes:

  • MIME and magic-byte validation on the uploaded evidence photo.
  • EXIF orientation normalization and metadata sanitization.
  • Persistent Cloudinary storage under folder urbanfix/evidence.
  • Storage in complaint.resolution_evidence and update of resolution_approvals.officer_submission = True.

19.2 Citizen Verification Gate (POST /api/complaints/{id}/reporter-verification)

The citizen who submitted the original report holds an unguessable reporter_verification_token issued upon intake. When work is uploaded, the citizen visits their private verification link and records an outcome:

  • fixed: Citizen confirms repair is satisfactory. resolution_approvals.reporter = True. If all approvals are met, complaint transitions to Resolved, emitting a citizen_confirmed audit event.
  • partially_fixed or not_fixed: Citizen reports that work was incomplete, substandard, or not performed. Status immediately transitions to Reopened - Needs Review. The complaint's priority score escalates, and an audit event complaint_reopened is broadcast.

19.3 Tamper-Evident SHA-256 Audit Chain (accountability.py)

Every administrative override, status transition, and evidence approval creates an immutable record in audit_events. Each record contains:

event["previous_hash"] = prior[-1].get("event_hash") if prior else None
event["event_hash"] = "sha256:" + sha256(canonical_json(event)).hexdigest()

The public accountability receipt (GET /api/track/{id}/receipt) summarizes all cluster incidents, AI assessments, priority breakdowns, SLA history, and chained audit hashes, generating a top-level receipt_hash that guarantees data integrity to civil society and municipal auditors.

22. Security and Privacy Considerations

22.1 Authentication & Credential Architecture

User credentials are encrypted using PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 with 310,000 iterations and 16-byte random salts. Sessions use 48-byte URL-safe cryptographic tokens. The raw session token is never stored in the database; only its SHA-256 digest (token_hash) is persisted. Sessions expire after 7 days via a MongoDB TTL index. The session token is transmitted in an urbanfix_session HTTP-only cookie with SameSite=Lax and Secure (enforced in production).

22.2 Double-Submit CSRF Defense

To defend against Cross-Site Request Forgery, every session issues an unguessable 24-byte random csrf_token. For every state-mutating request (POST, PATCH, DELETE), the backend verifies that the incoming X-CSRF-Token header matches the session CSRF token using hmac.compare_digest.

22.3 PII Redaction & Geospatial Privacy Engine (privacy.py)

Public accountability must not compromise citizen privacy. The backend enforces automated redacting rules:

  • Private Field Stripping: reporter_name, reporter_contact, source_fingerprint, affected_sources, internal_notes, private_metadata, and location_geo are deleted from all public responses.
  • Regex PII Redaction: Description and summary fields are scrubbed against verified regex patterns:
    • Bangladeshi Mobile: (?<!\d)(?:\+?880[-\s]?)?0?1[3-9]\d{8}(?!\d) → [phone redacted]
    • Bangladeshi NID: (?<!\d)(?:\d{10}|\d{13}|\d{17})(?!\d) → [identifier redacted]
    • Email Addresses: \b[A-Z0-9._%+-]+@[A-Z0-9.-]+\.[A-Z]{2,}\b → [email redacted]
  • Coordinate Fuzzing: Exact GPS coordinates on public feeds are rounded to 3 decimal places (~110 meters accuracy), preventing disclosure of exact home entrances.

22.4 Volunteer Safety Boundary Gate (volunteer_safety.py)

To safeguard youth and community volunteers from hazardous civic conditions, the system evaluates complaint descriptions against a dictionary of dangerous municipal terms (electrical wiring, high-voltage transformers, gas leaks, structural collapse, sewer biohazards, and unbarricaded manholes). Any matching issue is automatically marked ineligible for community participation, requiring certified municipal authority dispatch.

23. Error Handling and Validation

23.1 Pydantic Validation & HTTP Status Codes

All incoming requests are strictly validated using Pydantic schemas. The API emits explicit HTTP status codes:

  • 400 Bad Request: Malformed filename, missing parameters, or missing approved budget.
  • 401 Unauthorized: Missing authentication, invalid login credentials, or invalid webhook signature.
  • 403 Forbidden: Invalid CSRF token, cross-authority officer access attempt, or volunteer hazard rejection.
  • 404 Not Found: Entity (complaint, contractor, work offer) does not exist.
  • 409 Conflict: Invalid lifecycle transition, concurrent duplicate request, or already-upvoted post.
  • 413 Payload Too Large: Image upload exceeds upload limit (10MB dev / 4MB prod) or dimensions exceed 30 megapixels.
  • 415 Unsupported Media Type: File content type or magic bytes do not match permitted image formats.
  • 422 Unprocessable Entity: Schema validation failure, non-civic image upload, or invalid bounding box.
  • 429 Too Many Requests: Sliding-window rate limit breached.
  • 502 / 503 Service Unavailable: Upstream Cloudinary failure or database connection failure when fallback is disabled.

23.2 Multimodal AI Retry & Fallback Cascade

The Gemini client implements automated retry with exponential backoff on transient errors (HTTP 429, 500, 502, 503). If the API fails completely, times out (>90s), or returns an unparseable response, the system falls back to _fallback_analysis with deterministic keyword classification and sets needs_review=True.

28. Limitations

  • Heuristic Geographic Boundaries: While the ray-casting polygon algorithm is fully operational, candidate authority polygons currently approximate ward boundaries via bounding boxes pending official open GIS shapefile ingestion from the Bangladesh Local Government Division.
  • Commercial Payment Gateway Keys: Micro-grant community payouts run via simulated demo and Stripe test rails. Integration with bKash and Nagad disbursement APIs requires formal commercial merchant agreements.
  • Single Vision AI Model Dependency: Multimodal analysis requires internet connectivity to communicate with Google Gemini REST endpoints. When offline, the system safely falls back to text keyword heuristics but cannot visually inspect photos.
